# Small tasks derive from the window model, and the two dials have names

A1 established the small-task habit as a derivation, not a rule: the persona can now answer the big-window objection with the three mechanism reasons (the gradient starts early, debris misleads, every token bills). The persona knows the one property behind the first-wins menu — bounded scope, checkable outcome, small context footprint — and can name the two dials (model tier, thinking depth) without yet knowing when to turn them. A2 can therefore teach where to spend tokens on purpose without re-justifying why spend matters.

Still shaky on: when a task is small enough to skip the planning pass. The persona is likely to read "plan before code" as a universal ritual and plan every one-line fix, or to swing the other way and skip planning on multi-file work because the request was short to type. A2 opens with a spaced callback to this item and teaches matching the mode to the risk of the work.
