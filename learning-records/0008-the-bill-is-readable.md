# The bill is readable, and restarting is a costed decision

A7 closed record 0007's shaky item and with it Track A: the learner can now name the three warning signs of a degraded window (forgotten instructions, repeated approaches, confident wrong claims), and treats mid-task restarting as an economic decision instead of a defeat — spent tokens weigh nothing, the files carry the handoff, and the restart brief is only what never reached a file. The evidence is the finale: one feature built with every habit in sequence (save point zero, plan session, reviewed plan, fresh build session, tests as the done-check), then both bills read — the window view and the spend view — with the three questions answered from the learner's own numbers, compared against nobody.

Still shaky on: the fixed cost of session zero. Reading the window bill once surprised the learner — part of the window was full before the first word was typed (the tool's own machinery, the instructions file, the loaded add-ons) — but one look is not an audit. The learner cannot yet say what their own everyday setup loads into every single session, which add-on earns its slice, or how that floor compares to the transcript tax they just learned to shed. The efficiency course's first lesson (B1, audit sessions) opens exactly there: from reading one session's bill to auditing what every session pays before the work starts.
